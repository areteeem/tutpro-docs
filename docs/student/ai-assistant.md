# Student AI learning assistant

The Student AI assistant helps explain the current page, a lesson, or homework. It is designed to give hints, steps, and new examples—not direct answers to graded questions or completed work. It can still make mistakes, so use your teacher’s instructions as the authority.

## Ask about a page or assignment

1. Sign in to [Student](https://student.tutpro.org). The assistant appears when it is enabled for your account and the service is available.
2. Open the lesson or homework you want help with, then open **AI assistant**.
3. Ask a focused question, such as “What should I do first?” or “Can you explain this instruction in simpler words?”
4. If the assistant misunderstood the task, add the missing detail or ask it to explain one step at a time.
5. Use a linked lesson or homework card to return to the assignment. Complete and submit your own work in the normal task flow.

## Attach lesson or homework context

1. Open the lesson or assignment you want to discuss.
2. In the assistant, select **+** or **Add context**.
3. Choose **Attach this lesson** or **Attach this homework**. Remove the attachment if it is not relevant.
4. Ask your question. The assistant uses the selected context to explain the instructions and can link back to the related record.

## What the assistant uses and sends

To ground its response, the assistant can use the current page, your student profile details used by the app, relevant lesson and homework details, and any context you attach. The chat request is sent through TutPro’s server to its configured AI service; this is not on-device AI processing. Do not include passwords, private contact details, or other personal information in your message.

The assistant is instructed not to reveal answer keys or teacher-only notes, give direct answers to graded questions, or complete work for you. Ask it for a hint, a worked example with different numbers or wording, or a check of your reasoning instead. If it gives you a final answer or something seems wrong, do not submit it as your own—ask your teacher.
